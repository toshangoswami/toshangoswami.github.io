Badminton Shot Tracker

2026 computer vision Python · YOLOv8 · OpenCV · scikit-learn · Streamlit solo · independent project

Problem

I wanted tactical feedback on my own badminton, taken from my match footage: which shots I chose, where they went, and where my opponent stood. Advice built on a guess is worse than none, because it's confidently wrong and you can't tell. So every number had to be measured from the video, or marked as unmeasured.

What I built

A Streamlit app that analyses a match clip on a CPU. YOLOv8 finds the players, YOLOv8-pose tracks the near player's skeleton, and a spike in wrist speed marks each contact. A RandomForest classifies the shot from five frames of pose keypoints, then two physical checks can overrule it: contact height, and the shuttle's launch direction when it can be tracked. Rules flag shots that had a better option, and the whole session is read for one thing to work on next, with a drill.

Media

Result

Shot-selection feedback on your own match footage. The shot classifier reaches 89% accuracy, trained on hundreds of manually labelled frames.

Challenges

Consistent tracking was the hardest part. Footage from different camera angles also added too much variability for the prediction model to handle.
